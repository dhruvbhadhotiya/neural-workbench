The competition playbook: trust your validation

— · Lesson 4 of 4 · Schematic labs + one exact checklist

A leaderboard score is a noisy, single-number estimate. Your job is to build a local estimate you can trust, then spend a fixed time budget improving it without fooling yourself.

Objectives

  • Choose group, time or stratified folds that match how the test set was built.
  • Diagnose leakage from duplicates, target encoding and preprocessing outside the fold.
  • Use out-of-fold predictions for stacking, and average ensembles and TTA correctly.
  • Decide when pseudo-labelling helps and when it amplifies errors.

You know this from ML → here it becomes

In tabular ML, cross_val_score(model, X, y, cv=5) was usually enough. With images, rows are rarely independent: several slices come from one patient, frames from one video sit next to each other in time, and the test set may be a later time period. A random fold then trains on near-duplicates of its own validation rows.

Intuition: a score is only as good as its split

Start by reverse-engineering the competition: read the data description, look for repeated ids, filenames with timestamps, and identical or near-identical feature vectors. Build one validation scheme that mirrors the test distribution, then stop looking at the public leaderboard except as a final sanity check. Every design decision should be made against your local folds.

Three splitting rules cover most cases. GroupKFold keeps all rows sharing a group id (patient, video, image tile) in one fold. TimeSeriesSplit trains on the past and validates on a later window. StratifiedKFold protects class proportions when rows are genuinely independent. Fitting a scaler, target encoder or pseudo-label model before splitting leaks information across folds; all learned preprocessing must live inside the fold pipeline.

If a validation row has a near-duplicate in training, the score measures memorisation, not generalisation. Group and time splits cost a little accuracy on paper and buy a lot of truth.

The math

1 · Group and time CV sketches

Let \(g_i\) be the group id of row \(i\), and \(F_k\) the set of groups held out in fold \(k\). A valid fold satisfies

\[ i\in F_k \;\Longrightarrow\; \forall j:\ g_j=g_i \Rightarrow j\in F_k \]

Worked mini-example. Three patients A, B, C contribute two scans each: six rows. A random validation fold of two rows picks, say, A₁ and B₁. Their twins A₂ and B₂ stay in train, so 2 of 2 validation rows have a near-duplicate in training. GroupKFold on patient id instead holds out {A₁,A₂} in fold 1, {B₁,B₂} in fold 2, {C₁,C₂} in fold 3: zero duplicated patients cross the boundary.

For a time split, fold \(k\) trains on days \(1,\dots,k+2\) and validates on day \(k+3\). With five days available: fold 1 = train days 1–3 → val day 4; fold 2 = train 1–4 → val day 5. Training set sizes are 3 and 4 days, validation size 1 day each — no future information is ever used to predict the past.

2 · Ensemble variance falls as σ²/m

If \(m\) models make errors with variance \(\sigma^2\) and those errors are independent and equally weighted, the variance of their mean is

\[ \mathrm{Var}\!\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m} e_j\right)=\frac{\sigma^2}{m} \]

Worked example. With per-model error variance \(\sigma^2=0.04\) (\(\sigma=0.2\)) and \(m=5\): \(\sigma^2/m=0.04/5=0.008\), so the ensemble error standard deviation is \(\sqrt{0.008}\approx0.089\) — less than half of 0.2. If errors are correlated with coefficient \(\rho\), the correct expression is \(\rho\sigma^2+(1-\rho)\sigma^2/m\). At \(\rho=0.5\): \(0.5\cdot0.04+0.5\cdot0.008=0.024\), so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ens}}\approx0.155\). Diverse models matter as much as their count.

3 · TTA averaging and its rules

Test-time augmentation predicts the same image under \(T\) transforms (identity, horizontal flip, vertical flip, 180° rotation), maps each prediction back to the original orientation, then averages:

\[ \bar p_i = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} p_i^{(t)} \]

Worked example. One image yields probabilities \([0.70,0.76,0.64,0.90]\) for the four views. Their mean is \((0.70+0.76+0.64+0.90)/4=3.00/4=0.75\). The horizontal-flip prediction must be flipped back before averaging, or pixels are compared at different locations. Averaging probabilities (or logits) is allowed; averaging already-thresholded 0/1 labels is not.

4 · Pseudo-labelling risk

A model labels unlabelled data with confidence \(q\); you keep predictions above a threshold \(\tau\) and retrain. If \(n=1000\) unlabelled items are available, the model is confident on 90% of them and 10% of those confident predictions are wrong, then

\[ 0.90\cdot1000=900 \text{ kept},\qquad 0.10\cdot900=90 \text{ wrong labels injected} \]

Those 90 confident mistakes are exactly the samples the model is least likely to correct. Pseudo-labelling is worth trying only after the validation scheme is trustworthy, with a low enough threshold to add data but a high enough one to protect labels — and with a held-out set that never receives pseudo-labels.

Code

A competition skeleton: fold construction, a training loop with out-of-fold (OOF) predictions, a stacking step, TTA at inference and a submission file. All data here is synthetic; no downloads and no external state. 

import numpy as np, pandas as pd, torch, random
from sklearn.model_selection import GroupKFold, StratifiedKFold, TimeSeriesSplit
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression

SEED = 42
random.seed(SEED); np.random.seed(SEED); torch.manual_seed(SEED)

# ---- synthetic table: 60 images from 12 groups, no downloads ----------------
n = 60
X = np.random.randn(n, 8).astype("float32")
y = (X[:, 0] + 0.3 * np.random.randn(n) > 0).astype("int64")
groups = np.repeat(np.arange(12), 5)
df = pd.DataFrame(X); df["y"] = y; df["group"] = groups

# ---- folds: choose ONE scheme that mirrors the test distribution -----------
folds = GroupKFold(n_splits=5).split(X, y, groups)
# folds = StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=SEED).split(X, y)
# folds = TimeSeriesSplit(4).split(X)          # rows already sorted by time

def model():
    return torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(8, 16), torch.nn.ReLU(), torch.nn.Linear(16, 2))

def train_one(tr, va):
    net = model()
    opt = torch.optim.Adam(net.parameters(), lr=1e-2)
    xb, yb = torch.tensor(X[tr]), torch.tensor(y[tr])
    for _ in range(60):
        opt.zero_grad()
        loss = torch.nn.functional.cross_entropy(net(xb), yb)
        loss.backward(); opt.step()
    with torch.no_grad():
        return torch.softmax(net(torch.tensor(X[va])), dim=1).numpy()

# ---- out-of-fold predictions are the only honest stacking features ---------
oof = np.zeros((n, 2))
for tr, va in folds:                     # regenerate the fold iterator once if reused
    oof[va] = train_one(tr, va)
stacker = LogisticRegression().fit(oof, y)
print("OOF shape", oof.shape)            # (60, 2)

# ---- inference with TTA: invert each transform before averaging ------------
def predict_tta(net, image):
    views = [image, np.fliplr(image), np.flipud(image), np.rot90(image, 2)]
    ps = []
    for v, inv in zip(views, [lambda a: a, np.fliplr, np.flipud, lambda a: np.rot90(a, 2)]):
        with torch.no_grad():
            ps.append(torch.softmax(net(torch.tensor(inv(v))), dim=1).numpy())
    return np.mean(ps, axis=0)           # (K,) averaged probabilities

# ---- submission: ids and probabilities, no labels used after this point ----
sub = pd.DataFrame({"id": np.arange(n), "p": oof[:, 1]})
sub.to_csv("submission.csv", index=False)
print(sub.head(3))

The last gate before an upload is a fixed submission checklist: split audit, seed, OOF predictions, TTA inversion and a sanity check of the CSV. Keep it somewhere that survives a page reload, so a late-night run cannot skip a step.

An example 12-hour budget: 1 h data/split audit, 1.5 h baseline, 1.5 h model loop, 3 h improvements, 2 h OOF/ensemble/TTA, 1 h final checks, 2 h buffer. These are planning proportions, not performance claims.

Common mistakes

Public-LB overfitting

Checking every small change against the public leaderboard turns it into a second training set. Keep a private local fold for decisions and use the leaderboard once or twice near the end.

Leakage through target encoding

Computing a target mean on the full training set and then cross-validating gives every validation row a feature derived from its own label. Fit encoders inside each fold with out-of-fold values.

Inconsistent preprocessing

Training on normalised images but feeding raw pixels at inference silently changes the input distribution. Package transforms with the model and reuse the exact same pipeline.

No fixed seed, no sanity check

Unseeded splits and augmentation make two runs incomparable. Seed every RNG, then verify the submission file's shape, column order, id coverage and finite values before the final upload.

Debug this

Local 5-fold CV reports 0.95 accuracy; the public leaderboard reports 0.70. The model code, seed and preprocessing are identical in both. What is the first thing to investigate?

Show the likely cause and the fix

Group leakage. If many rows share a patient, video or original image, a random fold puts near-duplicates on both sides; the model recognises them instead of generalising. Count unique ids, hash the resized pixels to find exact duplicates, and rerun with GroupKFold on the right key. If the data is a time series, use TimeSeriesSplit instead — a future split is a different failure with the same symptom.

Second check: was a target-based feature or a scaler fitted before splitting? Both inflate CV while leaving the true test score untouched.

Check yourself

Summary

1 · Match folds to the test mechanism: group ids for repeated entities, expanding windows for time, stratified splits only for independent rows.
2 · OOF predictions are the only honest inputs to a stacker; ensembling m models cuts independent error variance to σ²/m (0.04/5 = 0.008), less when errors correlate.
3 · TTA averages un-transformed-back probabilities (0.70+0.76+0.64+0.90)/4 = 0.75; pseudo-labels and leaderboard peeking can turn a good local score into a bad submission.

Cheat sheet — M5·04

  • Split first, then fit any learned preprocessing inside each fold.
  • GroupKFold: all rows with the same \(g_i\) share a fold; TimeSeriesSplit never trains on the future.
  • Stacking features must be out-of-fold predictions.
  • Independent ensemble: \(\mathrm{Var}(\bar e)=\sigma^2/m\); correlated: \(\rho\sigma^2+(1-\rho)\sigma^2/m\).
  • TTA: invert the transform, then average probabilities — never average thresholds.
  • Seed every RNG; check submission id coverage, column order and finite values.
