U-Net, skips, and scoring masks

— · Lesson 2 of 4 · Exact math labs

An encoder shrinks the image until semantics are easy. A symmetric decoder grows it back — and skip connections return the pixels the pooling threw away.

Objectives

  • Trace every block of a 4-level U-Net: output shape and parameter count.
  • Explain what skip connections carry and why a shape mismatch is a real bug.
  • Train with a Dice + BCE objective and evaluate with IoU and Dice.
  • Handle empty masks without producing NaN, and know what smoothing implies.

You know this from ML → here it becomes

An autoencoder bottleneck compresses an image into a small vector. A U-Net keeps the same hourglass but replaces the single vector with a pyramid of feature maps, and shortcuts each encoder level to its decoder twin. Classification accuracy is replaced by IoU: a strict overlap score that punishes even a one-pixel shift.

Intuition: the hourglass with shortcuts

The encoder applies two \(3\times3\) convolutions at each level, then a \(2\times2\) max pool. Channels double as \(H,W\) halve. The bottleneck holds the most abstract features at the smallest grid. The decoder upsamples by 2, concatenates the matching encoder map, then applies two more convolutions. Finally a \(1\times1\) head turns channels into class logits.

Why skip? Pooling is information-destroying: a boundary that survives at \(256\times256\) is blurred into a coarse decision at \(16\times16\). Concatenation gives the decoder the original high-resolution channels back, so it can localise while using the bottleneck's semantic decisions. It must be a concatenation along the channel axis, not an addition — the two tensors carry different information.

Skips trade memory for detail: every saved encoder map is re-read by the decoder, which is why U-Nets are memory-hungry but extremely sample-efficient.

The math

1 · Shape trace for a 4-level U-Net

Config: input \((N,C,H,W)=(1,3,256,256)\), levels \(L=4\), base channels \(B=64\), classes \(=1\). Per level, channels are \(B\cdot2^i\); two \(3\times3\) convs with padding 1 keep \(H,W\); a \(2\times2/2\) pool halves them. The bottleneck has \(B\cdot2^4=1024\) channels. Each decoder upsample doubles \(H,W\), then the skip from encoder level \(i\) adds \(B\cdot2^i\) channels before two \(3\times3\) convs. All counts below are arithmetic from \(K^2 C_{in} C_{out}+C_{out}\) with \(K=3\), evaluated for each block.

Counts come from the displayed arithmetic, one \(K^2 C_{in}C_{out}+C_{out}\) per convolution. The decoder's first convolution is the easy one to get wrong: after concatenating, its input is \(1024+512=1536\) channels at dec3, not 1024.

Try an input size of 100 with three levels in the explorer: \(100\to50\to25\to12\) on the way down, then \(12\to24\) on the way up. The skip wants 25 rows but the decoder has 24. Padding the convolutions correctly does not fix this — the odd spatial size does. Crop the skip, pad the decoder, or choose sizes divisible by \(2^L\).

2 · IoU and Dice on a 6×6 toy mask

Let \(A\) be the predicted set of foreground pixels and \(B\) the ground-truth set:

\[ \mathrm{IoU}=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|},\qquad \mathrm{Dice}=\frac{2|A\cap B|}{|A|+|B|} \]

Worked example. Take a 6×6 grid with ground truth \(B=\) rows 1–3, columns 1–3 (a \(3\times3\) block, \(|B|=9\)) and prediction \(A=\) rows 1–3, columns 2–4 (shifted one column right, \(|A|=9\)):

6×6 toy mask · X = intersection, A = prediction only, B = truth only
row \ col012345
0......
1.BXXA.
2.BXXA.
3.BXXA.
4......
5......

Intersection: \(3\cdot2=6\) cells. Union: 9 + 9 − 6 = 12 cells.

\[ \mathrm{IoU}=6/12=0.5,\qquad \mathrm{Dice}=2\cdot6/(9+9)=12/18\approx0.667 \]

The identity \(\mathrm{Dice}=2\,\mathrm{IoU}/(1+\mathrm{IoU})\) checks out:

\[ 2(0.5)/(1+0.5)=1/1.5\approx0.667 \]

Dice is always at least IoU; both are 1 only for a perfect mask.

3 · Empty masks and smoothing

If \(A=B=\varnothing\), then \(|A|=|B|=|A\cup B|=0\) and both formulas are \(0/0\). A smoothed Dice with \(\epsilon=1\) is

\[ \mathrm{Dice}_{\epsilon}=\frac{2|A\cap B|+\epsilon}{|A|+|B|+\epsilon} \]

Worked cases with \(\epsilon=1\). Both masks empty: the numerator and denominator are both 1, so the score is 1 — the implementation declares two empty masks a perfect match. Prediction empty, truth of size 9: \(1/10=0.1\) instead of 0. Neither is magic; both are conventions you must state, because they change the metric on empty images.

4 · Dice + BCE objective

BCE gives well-calibrated probabilities but struggles when foreground is 1% of the image; Dice optimises overlap directly but has a noisy gradient on tiny objects. Their sum is the standard compromise:

\[ \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}} \] \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}=-\frac{1}{M}\sum_i\left[g_i\log p_i+(1-g_i)\log(1-p_i)\right] \] \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}=1-\frac{2\sum_i p_i g_i+\epsilon}{\sum_i p_i+\sum_i g_i+\epsilon} \]

Here \(M=H\cdot W\) is the number of pixels in one mask, \(p_i\in(0,1)\) the predicted probability and \(g_i\in\{0,1\}\) the target at pixel \(i\). Worked check: for one foreground pixel with \(p=0.8,g=1\), BCE contributes \(-\log0.8\approx0.223\); with \(p=0.2\) it contributes \(-\log0.2\approx1.609\). A Dice term weights the overlap of all \(M\) pixels at once.

Code

A compact U-Net trained on synthetic shapes generated in the file, with the combined loss. PyTorch comes first; the Keras tab shows the same architecture in NHWC internally (the course still describes tensors as NCHW). 

import torch
from torch import nn
from torch.nn import functional as F

def conv(cin, cout):
    return nn.Sequential(nn.Conv2d(cin, cout, 3, padding=1, bias=False),
                         nn.BatchNorm2d(cout), nn.ReLU(inplace=True))

class UNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, base=16, classes=1):
        super().__init__()
        ch = [base * 2**i for i in range(5)]
        self.enc = nn.ModuleList([conv(3 if i == 0 else ch[i-1], ch[i]) for i in range(4)])
        self.bot = nn.Sequential(conv(ch[4], ch[4]), conv(ch[4], ch[4]))
        self.dec = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Sequential(conv(ch[i] * 2, ch[i]), conv(ch[i], ch[i])) for i in range(3, -1, -1)
        ])
        self.head = nn.Conv2d(base, classes, 1)

    def forward(self, x):                       # x: (N, C, H, W)
        skips = []
        for block in self.enc:
            skips.append(block(x))              # save before pooling
            x = F.max_pool2d(skips[-1], 2)      # H,W -> H//2, W//2
        x = self.bot(x)
        for block, skip in zip(self.dec, reversed(skips)):
            x = F.interpolate(x, size=skip.shape[-2:], mode="bilinear", align_corners=False)
            x = torch.cat([skip, x], dim=1)     # concat on the CHANNEL axis
            x = block(x)
        return self.head(x)                     # logits: (N, classes, H, W)

def dice_loss(p, g, eps=1.0):                   # p: probabilities, g: target mask
    p, g = p.flatten(1), g.flatten(1)
    return 1 - (2 * (p * g).sum(1) + eps) / (p.sum(1) + g.sum(1) + eps)

def synthetic_batch(n=4, size=64):               # no downloads: squares on noise
    x = torch.rand(n, 3, size, size)
    yy, xx = torch.meshgrid(torch.arange(size), torch.arange(size), indexing="ij")
    g = ((xx >= 16) & (xx < 40) & (yy >= 16) & (yy < 40)).float()[None, None].repeat(n, 1, 1, 1)
    return x, g

x, g = synthetic_batch()
model, opt = UNet(), torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
for step in range(20):
    p = torch.sigmoid(model(x))
    loss = F.binary_cross_entropy(p, g) + dice_loss(p, g).mean()
    opt.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); opt.step()
print(float(loss))                               # shape (1, 1, 64, 64) masks throughout
import tensorflow as tf

def conv(cin, cout):
    return tf.keras.Sequential([
        tf.keras.layers.Conv2D(cout, 3, padding="same", use_bias=False),
        tf.keras.layers.BatchNormalization(), tf.keras.layers.Activation("relu"),
    ])

def unet(base=16):
    ch = [base * 2**i for i in range(5)]
    skips = []
    x = inp = tf.keras.Input(shape=(64, 64, 3))    # Keras uses NHWC internally
    for i in range(4):
        x = conv(3 if i == 0 else ch[i-1], ch[i])(x)
        skips.append(x)
        x = tf.keras.layers.MaxPooling2D(2)(x)
    x = conv(ch[4], ch[4])(conv(ch[4], ch[4])(x))
    for block, skip in zip(
        [tf.keras.Sequential([conv(ch[i]*2, ch[i]), conv(ch[i], ch[i])]) for i in range(3, -1, -1)],
        reversed(skips)):
        x = tf.keras.layers.UpSampling2D(2)(x)
        x = tf.keras.layers.Concatenate(axis=-1)([skip, x])  # channel axis is last here
        x = block(x)
    out = tf.keras.layers.Conv2D(1, 1)(x)                   # logits
    return tf.keras.Model(inp, out)

def dice_loss(g, logits, eps=1.0):
    p = tf.sigmoid(logits)
    g, p = tf.reshape(g, [-1]), tf.reshape(p, [-1])
    return 1 - (2*tf.reduce_sum(p*g) + eps) / (tf.reduce_sum(p) + tf.reduce_sum(g) + eps)

model = unet()
model.compile(optimizer=tf.keras.optimizers.Adam(1e-3),
              loss=lambda g, z: tf.keras.losses.binary_crossentropy(g, z, from_logits=True) + dice_loss(g, z))
print(model.count_params())

Use from_logits=True with raw logits, or apply sigmoid and use plain BCE — never both. Synthetic shapes here are generated in-file; no dataset is downloaded.

Common mistakes

Concatenating in the wrong order

\(\mathrm{cat}(\text{decoder},\text{skip})\) and \(\mathrm{cat}(\text{skip},\text{decoder})\) have the same shape but different channel semantics. Pick one convention, keep it in every block, and mirror it in inference code.

Pooling with ceil_mode mismatch

PyTorch's ceil_mode=True can produce 25 from 49 where floor gives 24. The decoder then upsamples to 48 or 50 and the skip no longer aligns. Keep one rounding rule.

BCE on logits without from_logits

Passing logits to a probability-loss applies no sigmoid, so negative values produce invalid logarithms. Apply sigmoid first or use the fused/from_logits form.

Unsmoothed Dice on empty masks

\(0/0\) yields NaN, and one NaN in a batch poisons the gradient for every image. Add \(\epsilon\), and decide explicitly whether two empty masks should score 1.

Debug this

The skip concatenation in the first decoder block throws sizes of tensors must match except in dimension 1. Got 64 and 32. The channel counts are correct; the spatial ones are not.

Show the likely cause and the fix

One encoder branch used \(3\times3\) convolutions with padding=0. Two such convs shrink each axis by 4 before the pool, so the saved skip is \(64\times64\) while the decoder, built from padded convs, is \(32\times32\). Use padding=1 on every \(3\times3\) in both branches (or crop the skip to the decoder size). Print each skip's shape after the encoder — that table is faster than reading the traceback.

Also verify odd input sizes: even with correct padding, \(100\to50\to25\to12\) breaks the upsample/skip alignment because 25 is odd.

Summary

1 · A U-Net halves resolution and doubles channels four times, then mirrors that path with skips that must match in space and channels.
2 · IoU = |A∩B|/|A∪B| and Dice = 2|A∩B|/(|A|+|B|) = 2IoU/(1+IoU); on the 6×6 shift they score 0.5 and 0.667.
3 · Dice+BCE balances overlap against calibration, and ε-smoothing prevents 0/0 — but it also declares two empty masks a perfect match.

Cheat sheet — M5·02

  • Conv params: \(K^2C_{in}C_{out}+C_{out}\); 2-level U-Net, base 16, 64² → 1 class: 118,273 total.
  • 4-level, base 64, 256²: 31,378,945 parameters (arithmetic above).
  • Skip channels after upsample at dec\(i\): \(B2^{i+1}+B2^{i}\).
  • \(\mathrm{IoU}=|A\cap B|/|A\cup B|\) and \(\mathrm{Dice}=2|A\cap B|/(|A|+|B|)\).
  • Identity: \(\mathrm{Dice}=2\,\mathrm{IoU}/(1+\mathrm{IoU})\).
  • Smoothed Dice: \((2|A\cap B|+\epsilon)/(|A|+|B|+\epsilon)\), \(\epsilon=1\).
  • Loss = BCE + Dice; logits need the fused BCE, probabilities need plain BCE.
