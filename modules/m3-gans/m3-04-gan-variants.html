GAN variants: what supervision do you have?

— · Lesson 4 of 4 · Illustrative walkthroughs

Paired images, unpaired domains, multiple attributes or no labels at all: each constraint produces a different GAN. Work through their objectives and know which one to reach for.

Objectives

  • Derive the cycle-consistency and identity losses and explain what each prevents.
  • Explain WGAN's 1-Lipschitz critic and compute a gradient-penalty term by hand.
  • Describe StarGAN's single generator and multi-head discriminator, plus PatchGAN and StyleGAN in one sentence each.
  • Place Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, StarGAN and unconditional GANs on the supervision axis.

You know this from ML → here it becomes

Conditional GANs need labels; a cGAN needs matched \((x,y)\) pairs. Real projects rarely have them. CycleGAN works with unpaired domains by demanding that going there and back returns you home; WGAN changes the loss to a distance with better-behaved gradients.

Intuition: a map of the variants

Pix2Pix needs paired images: for every sketch there is the exact photo. CycleGAN needs only two sets of images (horses, zebras) with no correspondences. StarGAN handles several domains with one generator conditioned on a target domain code. WGAN-GP is an unconditional training rule that replaces JS divergence with an Earth-Mover-style distance. StyleGAN restructures the generator for fine-grained control of styles at different scales.

Step through the walkthrough. The trick is that no paired photo is needed: a photo of a horse is translated to a zebra-like image, then back to a horse-like image, and the reconstruction is compared with the original. The adversarial losses make each output look native to its domain; the cycle loss keeps the content intact.

Pick a target domain and watch which discriminator head scores the result. The generator sees the input image and the target code; the discriminator sees the image and predicts both real/fake and the domain label. That is how one network serves many domains.

The math

1 · Cycle consistency

Let \(G:A\to B\) and \(F:B\to A\) be two generators. For \(x\sim p_A\) and \(y\sim p_B\):

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{cyc}}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl\lVert F(G(x))-x\bigr\rVert_1 +\mathbb{E}_{y}\bigl\lVert G(F(y))-y\bigr\rVert_1 \]

\(\lVert\cdot\rVert_1\) is the sum (here reported as the mean per element) of absolute differences. Worked example. With a three-pixel vector \(x=(1,0,-1)\) and \(F(G(x))=(0.8,0.1,-0.9)\):

\[ \lVert F(G(x))-x\rVert_1=\frac{|0.8-1|+|0.1-0|+|-0.9+1|}{3} =\frac{0.2+0.1+0.1}{3}=0.13333 \]

With \(\lambda_{\text{cyc}}=10\), the weighted contribution is \(10\cdot0.13333=1.33333\). The full CycleGAN objective adds the two adversarial terms:

\[ \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{GAN}}(G,D_B)+\mathcal{L}_{\text{GAN}}(F,D_A) +\lambda_{\text{cyc}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyc}} +\lambda_{\text{id}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{id}} \]

Worked example. If the two adversarial losses are 0.71 and 0.68 and the weighted cycle term is 1.33333 (with no identity term), the generator objective is \(0.71+0.68+1.33333=2.72333\). The identity loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{id}}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p_B}\lVert G(y)-y\rVert_1\) keeps colour and texture stable when the input is already in the target domain. Worked example. If \(y=(0.5,-0.5)\) and \(G(y)=(0.4,-0.3)\), then \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{id}}=(0.1+0.2)/2=0.15\), and with \(\lambda_{\text{id}}=5\) it contributes 0.75.

2 · WGAN and the gradient penalty

\[ \max_{\lVert f\rVert_L\le 1}\; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim p_r}\bigl[f(\mathbf{x})\bigr] -\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\sim p_g}\bigl[f(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\bigr] \]

\(f\) is a critic, not a probability output — its final sigmoid is removed. It must be 1-Lipschitz, meaning its gradient norm is at most 1 everywhere. WGAN-GP enforces this with a penalty on random interpolates \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\alpha\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha)\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\), \(\alpha\sim U(0,1)\):

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{GP}}=\lambda_{\text{GP}}\, \mathbb{E}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}\Bigl[\bigl(\lVert\nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} f(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\rVert_2-1\bigr)^2\Bigr] \]

Worked example. Take a real scalar \(x=1\), a fake \(\tilde x=0.2\), and \(\alpha=0.5\): the interpolate is \(\hat x=0.5\cdot1+0.5\cdot0.2=0.6\). If the critic is locally \(f(x)=2x-1\), then \(f'(x)=2\), \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert_2=2\), and with \(\lambda_{\text{GP}}=10\) the penalty is \(10(2-1)^2=10\). The critic loss to minimise is \(-\mathbb{E}_r f+\mathbb{E}_g f+\mathcal{L}_{\text{GP}}\).

Another worked check of the critic term: if \(f\) gives 1.0 and 0.8 on two real samples and \(-0.6\) and \(-0.2\) on two fake samples, then \(\mathbb{E}_r f=(1.0+0.8)/2=0.9\), \(\mathbb{E}_g f=(-0.6-0.2)/2=-0.4\), and the estimated distance is \(0.9-(-0.4)=1.3\). A critic with BatchNorm breaks the gradient penalty, so use GroupNorm or no normalisation and never weight-clip together with GP.

3 · StarGAN and the domain-label trick

One generator \(G(\mathbf{x},c)\) takes an image and a target domain code \(c\) (often one-hot over \(N_{\text{domains}}\)). The discriminator \(D\) outputs a real/fake logit and a domain logit vector. Its classification loss teaches \(G\) to produce the requested domain:

\[ \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}+\lambda_{\text{cls}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} +\lambda_{\text{rec}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}} \]

Worked example. With four domains the target is \(c=(0,1,0,0)\). If the discriminator's domain logits are \((0.1,2.0,-0.5,0.3)\), the softmax probabilities are \((0.1058,0.7070,0.0580,0.1292)\), so the cross-entropy is \(-\log 0.7070=0.34666\). The reconstruction term \(G(G(x,c),c_{\text{orig}})\) replaces CycleGAN's two generators with a single round trip.

4 · PatchGAN and StyleGAN in one paragraph each

A PatchGAN discriminator does not output one number per image: it outputs an \(H'\times W'\) map where each cell judges one patch, encouraging sharp local texture. Worked example. A 64×64 image with a discriminator whose receptive field is 16×16 and stride 16 gives a 4×4 decision map, i.e. 16 patch decisions.

A StyleGAN generator first maps \(\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{512}\) through a mapping network to \(\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{512}\), then injects \(\mathbf{w}\) as a style at each resolution via adaptive instance normalisation. Early styles control coarse shape, late styles control fine texture; \(W\) is less entangled with the training data than \(Z\).

Symbols used in this lesson

\(G:A\to B\), \(F:B\to A\) two generators · \(D_A,D_B\) domain discriminators · \(x\sim p_A\), \(y\sim p_B\) unpaired samples · \(\lambda_{\text{cyc}},\lambda_{\text{id}}\), \(\lambda_{\text{cls}},\lambda_{\text{rec}},\lambda_{\text{GP}}\) loss weights · \(f\) WGAN critic · \(p_r,p_g\) real and generated distributions · \(\alpha\sim U(0,1)\) interpolation coefficient · \(\hat x\) interpolate · \(c\) target-domain code · \(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}\) StyleGAN latent and mapped style · \(N_{\text{domains}}\) number of domains.

Code

Module definitions and shape prints for a CycleGAN-style ResNet generator, a PatchGAN discriminator, and the WGAN-GP gradient penalty. Nothing is downloaded; the tensors are random so you can check the shapes and the penalty value immediately. 

import torch
from torch import nn
from torch.autograd import grad

torch.manual_seed(0)

class ResNetBlock(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, c):
        super().__init__()
        self.block = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(c, c, 3, padding=1, bias=False), nn.InstanceNorm2d(c), nn.ReLU(True),
            nn.Conv2d(c, c, 3, padding=1, bias=False), nn.InstanceNorm2d(c),
        )
    def forward(self, x):
        return torch.relu(x + self.block(x))       # skip connection

class CycleGenerator(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, c_in=3, c_out=3, n_blocks=6, width=64):
        super().__init__()
        layers = [nn.Conv2d(c_in, width, 7, padding=3, bias=False),
                  nn.InstanceNorm2d(width), nn.ReLU(True)]
        h, w = width, width
        for _ in range(2):                          # 64 -> 32 -> 16
            layers += [nn.Conv2d(h, w * 2, 3, stride=2, padding=1, bias=False),
                       nn.InstanceNorm2d(w * 2), nn.ReLU(True)]
            h, w = w * 2, w * 2
        layers += [ResNetBlock(h) for _ in range(n_blocks)]
        for _ in range(2):                          # 16 -> 32 -> 64
            layers += [nn.ConvTranspose2d(h, h // 2, 3, stride=2, padding=1,
                                          output_padding=1, bias=False),
                       nn.InstanceNorm2d(h // 2), nn.ReLU(True)]
            h //= 2
        layers += [nn.Conv2d(h, c_out, 7, padding=3), nn.Tanh()]
        self.net = nn.Sequential(*layers)
    def forward(self, x):
        return self.net(x)                          # (N, 3, 64, 64)

class PatchGAN(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, c_in=3):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(c_in, 64, 4, stride=2, padding=1), nn.LeakyReLU(0.2, True),
            nn.Conv2d(64, 128, 4, stride=2, padding=1, bias=False),
            nn.InstanceNorm2d(128), nn.LeakyReLU(0.2, True),
            nn.Conv2d(128, 256, 4, stride=2, padding=1, bias=False),
            nn.InstanceNorm2d(256), nn.LeakyReLU(0.2, True),
            nn.Conv2d(256, 512, 4, stride=1, padding=1, bias=False),
            nn.InstanceNorm2d(512), nn.LeakyReLU(0.2, True),
            nn.Conv2d(512, 1, 4, stride=1, padding=1),  # one logit per patch
        )
    def forward(self, x):
        return self.net(x)                           # (N, 1, H', W')

G_AB = CycleGenerator()
D_B = PatchGAN()
x = torch.randn(2, 3, 64, 64)
fake_b = G_AB(x)
print("input", tuple(x.shape), "fake", tuple(fake_b.shape),
      "patches", tuple(D_B(fake_b).shape))            # (2,1,4,4) for a 64x64 input

def gradient_penalty(critic, real, fake, lambda_gp=10.0):
    """WGAN-GP: penalise ||grad f||_2 away from 1 on interpolates."""
    alpha = torch.rand(real.size(0), 1, 1, 1, device=real.device)
    inter = (alpha * real + (1 - alpha) * fake.detach()).requires_grad_(True)
    d = critic(inter)
    g = grad(outputs=d, inputs=inter,
             grad_outputs=torch.ones_like(d), create_graph=True)[0]
    return lambda_gp * ((g.norm(p=2, dim=1) - 1) ** 2).mean()

# A scalar sanity check of the formula from the lesson: real=1, fake=0.2, alpha=0.5.
r = torch.full((4, 1, 1, 1), 1.0)
f = torch.full((4, 1, 1, 1), 0.2)
lin = nn.Conv2d(1, 1, 1)                            # f(x) = w*x + b
with torch.no_grad():
    lin.weight.fill_(2.0); lin.bias.fill_(-1.0)
print("GP (expect 10 with alpha=0.5):",
      round(gradient_penalty(lin, r, f, 10.0).item(), 4))

The GP sanity print depends on the random \(\alpha\) values, so it will not be exactly 10 unless every sampled \(\alpha\) is 0.5 — the worked example uses that single case. The critic deliberately has no BatchNorm: normalisation within a sample breaks the gradient penalty.

Common mistakes

\(\lambda_{\text{cyc}}\) far too high

If cycle consistency dominates, the generator learns the identity map: the output resembles the input and no style transfer happens. Lower the weight until content is preserved but the domain changes.

No identity loss

Without \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{id}}\), feeding an image already in the target domain can shift its colours. Identity mapping acts as a gentle colour-preservation prior.

GP on the wrong interpolates

Interpolate between real and generated samples, not between two reals. Penalising the wrong distribution enforces the constraint where it is not needed.

BatchNorm in the critic

BatchNorm couples samples inside a batch and destroys the clean pointwise gradient norm the penalty measures. Use no normalisation or GroupNorm, and do not also weight-clip.

Debug this

A reader reports: “my CycleGAN output is almost identical to the input — no style change at all.” The adversarial losses are not zero.

Show the likely cause and the fix

The cycle weight \(\lambda_{\text{cyc}}\) is too large. When the identity map minimises the round-trip error perfectly, the optimiser has no reason to change the appearance: the adversarial terms are too weak to overcome the cycle penalty. Try reducing \(\lambda_{\text{cyc}}\) from 10 toward 1, check that the two domain discriminators are not already at 100% accuracy, and inspect samples at several checkpoints.

Second possibility: the generators were accidentally tied or the same domain discriminator was used for both sides, which also produces an identity solution.

Summary

1 · The supervision axis runs Pix2Pix (paired) → CycleGAN (unpaired domains) → StarGAN (many domains) → unconditional GANs.
2 · CycleGAN's cycle and identity losses preserve content and colour; WGAN-GP trades the JS game for a 1-Lipschitz critic and a gradient penalty.
3 · PatchGAN judges local texture with a map of patch decisions, while StyleGAN's mapping network gives coarse-to-fine style control.

Cheat sheet — M3·04

  • Cycle: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{cyc}}=\mathbb{E}\lVert F(G(x))-x\rVert_1+\mathbb{E}\lVert G(F(y))-y\rVert_1\).
  • Worked cycle error: (0.2+0.1+0.1)/3 = 0.13333; with λ=10 → 1.33333.
  • WGAN: \(\max_{\lVert f\rVert_L\le1}\mathbb{E}_r f-\mathbb{E}_g f\); GP = λ E[(‖∇f‖₂−1)²] on interpolates.
  • Worked GP: x=1, x̃=0.2, α=0.5 → x̂=0.6; f′=2 → 10(2−1)²=10.
  • StarGAN: G(x,c), D predicts real/fake plus domain; losses adv + cls + rec.
  • PatchGAN: one real/fake decision per patch. StyleGAN: z → w mapping + per-resolution styles.
