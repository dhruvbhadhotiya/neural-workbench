Linear algebra, chain rule and probability

— · Lesson 6 of 7 · Exact math labs

Every formula this course uses, written once with consistent symbols and one worked numeric example each. Keep this page open while reading the other modules.

Objectives

  • Look up the derivative of a scalar or matrix expression without re-deriving it.
  • Compose Jacobians in the correct order and predict a linear map's effect on area.
  • Relate entropy, KL divergence and cross-entropy with numbers.
  • Check attention and softmax shapes and derivatives before debugging code.

You know this from ML → here it becomes

The same linear algebra and probability you used for logistic regression still apply — but now the objects are matrices of shape \((N,C,H,W)\) and the chain rule is a product of Jacobians. Getting the transpose or the order wrong is the most common silent bug in a hand-written backward pass.

Intuition: derivatives are local linear maps

A derivative is not a number in general; it is the matrix of a linear approximation at a point. For a scalar loss the gradient is a vector; for a vector-valued function the derivative is a Jacobian. Composing functions composes their local linear maps — which means matrix multiplication, in the same order as the functions were composed.

That geometric view makes two facts obvious. First, a Jacobian applied to the unit square turns it into a parallelogram, whose area is scaled by \(|\det J|\). Second, the derivative of a softmax is not diagonal: changing one logit moves every probability.

Write the shape of every derivative next to it. A transpose error usually survives the forward pass and fails only in the backward pass — with a shape error or, worse, with silently wrong gradients of the same shape.

The math

1 · Matrix-calculus identities

\(f=a^\top x\)\(\partial f/\partial x=a\)\((D)\)
\(f=x^\top A x\)\((A+A^\top)x\)\((D)\)
\(f=\lVert x\rVert_2^2\)\(2x\)\((D)\)

Here \(x,a\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\), \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}\), and \(AB\) is square so the trace is defined. "Denominator layout" means the gradient has the shape of the denominator (the thing you differentiate with respect to).

Worked examples. (i) \(a=(2,3)\), \(x=(1,4)\): \(a^\top x=2\cdot1+3\cdot4=14\) and \(\partial f/\partial x=(2,3)\). (ii) \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\0&3\end{bmatrix}\), \(x=(1,2)\): \(f=x_1^2+2x_1x_2+3x_2^2=1+4+12=17\); \(A+A^\top=\begin{bmatrix}2&2\\2&6\end{bmatrix}\) and \((A+A^\top)x=(2+4,\;2+12)=(6,14)\), which matches direct differentiation. (iii) \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\), \(B=\begin{bmatrix}5&6\\7&8\end{bmatrix}\): \(AB=\begin{bmatrix}19&22\\43&50\end{bmatrix}\), \(\operatorname{tr}(AB)=19+50=69\), and \(\partial f/\partial A=B^\top=\begin{bmatrix}5&7\\6&8\end{bmatrix}\).

2 · Jacobian chain rule

\[ J_{f\circ g}(x) = J_f\!\left(g(x)\right)\,J_g(x) \]

\(g:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2\) is applied first, \(f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2\) second. The product is evaluated at \(g(x)\) for the outer Jacobian and at \(x\) for the inner one — never the other way round. A gradient vector is the special case \(\nabla_x(h\circ g)=J_g(x)^\top\nabla_h\,h(g(x))\).

Worked example. Let \(g(x,y)=(x^2,xy)\) and \(f(u,v)=(u+v,uv)\), evaluated at \(x=(1,2)\). Then \(g(1,2)=(1,2)\) and

\[ J_g(1,2)=\begin{bmatrix}2x&0\\y&x\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\2&1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad J_f(1,2)=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\v&u\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\2&1\end{bmatrix} \]

Multiplying in composition order:

\[ J_{f\circ g}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\2&1\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}2&0\\2&1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}4&1\\6&1\end{bmatrix} \]

Check by direct substitution: \(f(g(x,y))=(x^2+xy,\;x^3y)\), whose derivative with respect to \(x\) is \((2x+y,\;3x^2y)=(4,6)\) at \((1,2)\) — exactly the first column of the product. Its derivative with respect to \(y\) is \((x,\;x^3)=(1,1)\), the second column. Writing the shapes beside each factor turns this into a two-minute check.

Unit square. A matrix \(J=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\10&2\end{bmatrix}\) sends the corners \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) to \((0,0),(4,10),(1,2),(5,12)\): the square becomes a parallelogram of area \(|\det J|=|4\cdot2-1\cdot10|=2\). The lab below draws this map for several small compositions.

3 · Entropy, KL and cross-entropy

\[ H(p)=-\sum_i p_i\ln p_i,\qquad D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)=\sum_i p_i\ln\frac{p_i}{q_i},\qquad \mathrm{CE}(p,q)=H(p)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)=-\sum_i p_i\ln q_i \]

\(p\) is the true or target distribution, \(q\) the model's distribution, and all logs are natural (nats). \(H(p)\ge0\), \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)\ge0\), with equality iff \(p=q\); KL is not symmetric.

Worked example. With \(p=(0.5,0.5)\) and \(q=(0.75,0.25)\): \(H(p)=-2(0.5\ln0.5)=\ln2=0.6931\). Then \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)=0.5\ln(0.5/0.75)+0.5\ln(0.5/0.25) =0.5(-0.4055)+0.5(0.6931)=0.1438\). So \(\mathrm{CE}=0.6931+0.1438=0.8369\), which also equals \(-0.5\ln0.75-0.5\ln0.25=0.1438+0.6931\). Reversing the arguments gives \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\Vert p)=0.75\ln1.5+0.25\ln0.5=0.3041-0.1733=0.1308\neq0.1438\).

4 · Softmax Jacobian

\[ p=\mathrm{softmax}(z),\qquad \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad J=p(\mathbf{1}-p)^\top\ \text{on the diagonal adjustments} \]

\(z\) is the vector of logits, \(p\) the probabilities, and \(\delta_{ij}=1\) when \(i=j\) and \(0\) otherwise. The full Jacobian is \(\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\). Its rows sum to zero because the probabilities sum to one.

Worked example. With \(p=(0.6,0.3,0.1)\): \(\partial p_1/\partial z_1=0.6(1-0.6)=0.24\); \(\partial p_1/\partial z_2=0.6(0-0.3)=-0.18\); \(\partial p_2/\partial z_2=0.3(1-0.3)=0.21\). Row 1 sums to \(0.24-0.18-0.06=0\), as required. Combined with cross-entropy, \(\partial\mathcal L/\partial z=p-y\), the coupling disappears in the fused gradient.

5 · Batch-norm Jacobian (sketch)

\[ \hat x_i=\frac{x_i-\mu_B}{\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\epsilon}},\qquad \frac{\partial \hat x_i}{\partial x_j} =\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\epsilon}}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{1}{N}-\frac{\hat x_i\hat x_j}{N}\right) \]

\(N\) is the batch size, \(\mu_B,\sigma_B^2\) its mean and variance, and \(\hat x_i\) a normalised activation. With learnable scale \(\gamma\), multiply the matrix by \(\gamma\).

Worked example. One channel, \(N=2\), \(x=(1,3)\), \(\mu_B=2\), \(\sigma_B=1\), \(\gamma=1\), so \(\hat x=(-1,1)\). Then \(\partial\hat x_1/\partial x_1=1-1/2-((-1)(-1))/2=0\) and \(\partial\hat x_1/\partial x_2=0-1/2-((-1)(1))/2=0\). Normalising two numbers to \((-1,1)\) makes the result invariant to their common shift and scale — a useful sanity check on any BN backward implementation.

6 · Attention shapes

\[ \mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V \]

\(Q,K,V\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times H_{\text{heads}}\times T\times d_k}\), with batch \(N\), heads \(H_{\text{heads}}\), sequence length \(T\) and key width \(d_k=D/H_{\text{heads}}\). Scores have shape \((N,H_{\text{heads}},T,T)\) and softmax is over the last axis.

Worked example. With \(N=2\), \(H_{\text{heads}}=4\), \(T=16\), \(d_k=8\): \(Q\) is \((2,4,16,8)\), \(K^\top\) is \((2,4,8,16)\), and \(QK^\top\) is \((2,4,16,16)\), i.e. \(2\cdot4\cdot16\cdot16=2048\) score entries. Multiplying by \(V\;(2,4,16,8)\) gives \((2,4,16,8)\); concatenating heads yields \((2,16,32)\).

Common mistakes

Transposing in the chain rule

For a vector gradient use \(J_g^\top\nabla h\), not \(J_g\nabla h\). The shapes often still line up for square Jacobians, which is why this bug hides.

Reversing the Jacobian product

\(J_{f\circ g}=J_f J_g\): the function applied last goes on the left. Matrix products do not commute, so order is a correctness issue.

Confusing KL directions

\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)\neq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\Vert p)\): with \(p=(0.5,0.5)\), \(q=(0.75,0.25)\) the values are \(0.1438\) and \(0.1308\).

CE is not entropy

\(\mathrm{CE}=H(p)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)\). For a one-hot target \(H(p)=0\), but for a soft target the entropy term does not vanish.

Debug this

A hand-written backward pass for \(f(g(x))\) has the right shapes but wrong numbers. The code multiplies \(J_g J_f\) and calls the result \(\partial f/\partial x\).

Show the likely cause and the fix

The product is reversed. Since \(g\) is applied first and \(f\) second, \(\partial(f\circ g)/\partial x=J_f(g(x))\,J_g(x)\); the outer Jacobian must be evaluated at \(g(x)\) and placed on the left. Square matrices make the wrong product shape-compatible, so a shape print will not catch it. Verify with a finite-difference check on one component.

If the Jacobian is not square, the wrong order usually throws a shape error — which is the lucky case. Always write the shapes of both factors in a comment.

Summary

1 · Denominator-layout identities: \(\partial(a^\top x)/\partial x=a\), \(\partial(x^\top Ax)/\partial x=(A+A^\top)x\), \(\partial\operatorname{tr}(AB)/\partial A=B^\top\).
2 · Composition multiplies Jacobians as \(J_f J_g\): \([[1,1],[2,1]][[2,0],[2,1]]=[[4,1],[6,1]]\).
3 · \(\mathrm{CE}=H(p)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)\), softmax \(J=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\), attention scores \((N,H,T,T)\).

Cheat sheet — M6·06

  • \(\partial(a^\top x)/\partial x=a\); \(\partial(x^\top Ax)/\partial x=(A+A^\top)x\); \(\partial\lVert x\rVert^2/\partial x=2x\).
  • \(J_{f\circ g}=J_f(g(x))J_g(x)\); vector chain rule \(J_g^\top\nabla h\); area scales by \(|\det J|\).
  • \(H(p)=-\sum p\ln p\); \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)=\sum p\ln(p/q)\); \(\mathrm{CE}=H+D_{\mathrm{KL}}\).
  • Softmax: \(\partial p_i/\partial z_j=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j)\); \(J=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\).
  • Attention: \((N,H,T,d_k)\times(N,H,d_k,T)\to(N,H,T,T)\); softmax over last axis.
