GPT and decoding: from logits to text

— · Lesson 4 of 5 · Exact math labs

A language model only supplies a distribution over the next token. Everything that feels like “creativity” or “quality” happens in the decoding rule you apply to that distribution.

Objectives

  • Explain causal masking and the autoregressive factorisation \(p(x_{1:T})=\prod_t p(x_t\mid x_{<t})\).
  • Define perplexity and compute it from a short token sequence.
  • Apply temperature, top-\(k\) and top-\(p\) to a fixed 5-token distribution, with renormalisation.
  • Compare greedy, beam search and sampling, and know when each fails.

You know this from ML → here it becomes

BERT's encoder reads a whole sequence at once. A GPT-style decoder can only read what came before: a causal mask zeroes every attention weight to future positions. The same softmax layer then produces a probability over the vocabulary, and a decoding rule — not the model alone — decides which token is emitted. Logits are the pre-softmax scores; all truncation and temperature operations act on them or on the resulting distribution.

Intuition: one token at a time

Let \(T\) be the sequence length and \(x_t\) the token at position \(t\). Training maximises the likelihood of each token given its prefix. At inference, the model computes logits \(\mathbf z_t\in\mathbb R^{|V|}\), a distribution is formed, one token is selected, and the process repeats. The key/value tensors of earlier layers can be cached (the KV cache) so that each new step does not recompute the whole prefix.

Greedy decoding takes the argmax — deterministic, but prone to dull or repetitive text. Beam search keeps \(B\) partial sequences, which can also be bland. Sampling draws from the distribution, which is livelier but can produce nonsense tokens. Temperature, top-\(k\) and top-\(p\) are controls on that distribution.

Temperature reshapes the whole distribution; top-\(k\) keeps a fixed number of tokens; top-\(p\) keeps a variable number whose cumulative mass reaches \(p\). Every truncation must be followed by renormalisation so the probabilities still sum to one.

The math

1 · Autoregressive factorisation

The joint probability of a token sequence factors into conditionals one token at a time:

\[ p(x_{1:T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p(x_t\mid x_{1:t-1}) =p(x_1)\,p(x_2\mid x_1)\cdots p(x_T\mid x_{1:T-1}) \]

The causal mask enforces exactly this dependency: position \(t\) cannot attend to positions \(>t\), so its prediction is a function of the prefix only. Here \(x_{1:t-1}\) denotes tokens \(1\) through \(t-1\), and the empty prefix conditions \(p(x_1)\).

Worked example. For “the cat sat”, let \(p(\text{the})=0.4\), \(p(\text{cat}\mid\text{the})=0.3\), and \(p(\text{sat}\mid\text{the cat})=0.2\). Then \(p(\text{the cat sat})=0.4\times0.3\times0.2=0.024\), i.e. 2.4% under this toy model. This is a chain rule identity, not an approximation.

2 · Perplexity

Perplexity is the exponential of the mean negative log-likelihood in nats. With \(T\) tokens:

\[ \mathrm{PPL}(x_{1:T})=\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p(x_t\mid x_{1:t-1})\right) \]

It can be read as the effective number of equally likely tokens the model is choosing between per step. Lower is better, and it is only comparable across models sharing a tokenizer.

Worked example. Using the sequence above, the total negative log-likelihood is \(-\log(0.024)=-(\log0.4+\log0.3+\log0.2)=0.9163+1.2040+1.6094=3.7297\) nats. The mean is \(3.7297/3=1.2432\), and \(\mathrm{PPL}=e^{1.2432}=3.467\). Note that this equals the reciprocal of the geometric mean of the three token probabilities.

3 · Temperature softmax on 5 logits

Temperature \(\tau>0\) divides the logits before softmax, which reshapes the distribution without changing the ranking:

\[ \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf z/\tau)_i=\frac{e^{z_i/\tau}}{\sum_{j}e^{z_j/\tau}} \]

\(\tau=1\) is the original distribution; \(\tau<1\) sharpens it toward the argmax; \(\tau>1\) flattens it. As \(\tau\to0\) this tends to greedy decoding (not a uniform distribution); as \(\tau\to\infty\) it tends to uniform.

Worked example. Take the fixed toy logits used by the lab, \(\mathbf z=(2,1,0.5,0,-1)\), for tokens (the, cat, sat, on, mat).

  • \(\tau=1\): exponentials \((7.389,2.718,1.649,1.000,0.368)\), sum \(13.124\); probabilities \((0.5630,0.2071,0.1256,0.0762,0.0280)\).
  • \(\tau=0.5\): scaled logits \((4,2,1,0,-2)\), exponentials \((54.598,7.389,2.718,1.000,0.135)\), sum \(65.840\); probabilities \((0.8292,0.1122,0.0413,0.0152,0.0021)\).

Halving the temperature raises the top token's probability from \(0.5630\) to \(0.8292\): sharper, less varied text.

4 · top-\(k\), top-\(p\) and renormalisation

Top-\(k\) keeps the \(k\) largest probabilities and zeroes the rest. Top-\(p\) (nucleus) keeps the smallest set of highest-probability tokens whose cumulative mass reaches \(p\), then divides every kept probability by the kept mass \(S\) so they sum to one again:

\[ q_i=\begin{cases}p_i/S,& i\in K_{\text{kept}}\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}, \qquad S=\sum_{i\in K_{\text{kept}}}p_i \]

Here \(K_{\text{kept}}\) is the kept index set and \(S\le 1\) is its retained probability mass. In code, apply temperature first, then top-\(k\), then top-\(p\).

Worked example. Start from the \(\tau=1\) probabilities above, sorted \((0.5630,0.2071,0.1256,0.0762,0.0280)\), and set top-\(p=0.8\). The cumulative masses are \(0.5630\), \(0.7701\), \(0.8958\), …; two tokens are not enough (\(0.7701<0.8\)), so three are kept and \(S=0.8958\). Renormalised: \(0.5630/0.8958=0.6285\), \(0.2071/0.8958=0.2312\), \(0.1256/0.8958=0.1402\). These sum to \(0.9999\) up to rounding — an unnormalised distribution would bias the seeded draws.

5 · Beam score

Beam search keeps the \(B\) best partial sequences. To compare candidates of different lengths, score by the mean log-probability (length-normalised):

\[ s(y_{1:L})=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\log p(y_\ell\mid y_{1:\ell-1}) \]

\(L\) is the candidate length and \(y_{1:L}\) the candidate token sequence. A higher score (less negative) is better.

Worked example. Candidate A has token probabilities \((0.4,0.3,0.2)\): log-probabilities \((-0.9163,-1.2040,-1.6094)\), mean \(-1.2432\). Candidate B has \((0.1,0.9,0.5)\): logs \((-2.3026,-0.1054,-0.6931)\), mean \(-1.0337\). Beam search prefers B even though greedy decoding at the first step would have picked A's \(0.4\) over B's \(0.1\).

Code

First a 30-line sampler over the toy distribution: it uses the same order of operations as the lab and prints the renormalised probabilities. Then the equivalent Hugging Face generate() call with the same parameters; the model download and vocabulary are the reader's responsibility in Colab. Shapes are \((N,|V|)\) for batches of logits and \((T)\) for a sampled token sequence. 

import torch

torch.manual_seed(7)
logits = torch.tensor([2.0, 1.0, 0.5, 0.0, -1.0])   # shape (|V|=5,), fixed
tokens = ["the", "cat", "sat", "on", "mat"]
temperature, top_k, top_p = 1.0, 3, 0.8

p = torch.softmax(logits / temperature, dim=-1)     # (5,) sums to 1
# top-k: keep the k largest, zero the rest, renormalise
vals, idx = torch.topk(p, top_k)                    # both (3,)
mask = torch.zeros_like(p, dtype=torch.bool)
mask[idx] = True
p = torch.where(mask, p, torch.zeros_like(p))
p = p / p.sum()

# top-p: keep the shortest prefix reaching cumulative mass top_p, renormalise
order = torch.argsort(p, descending=True)
cum = torch.cumsum(p[order], dim=-1)
n_keep = int(torch.searchsorted(cum, top_p) + 1)    # 3 for the worked example
keep = torch.zeros_like(p, dtype=torch.bool)
keep[order[:n_keep]] = True
p = torch.where(keep, p, torch.zeros_like(p))
p = p / p.sum()                                     # sums to 1 again

draws = torch.multinomial(p, 200, replacement=True) # (200,) token indices
hist = torch.bincount(draws, minlength=len(tokens)) # (5,) counts
print([round(v, 4) for v in p.tolist()])
print({tokens[i]: int(hist[i]) for i in range(len(tokens))})
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM
import torch

name = "gpt2"                                  # any causal LM checkpoint
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(name)
model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(name)
model.eval()

ids = tok("The cat sat on the", return_tensors="pt").input_ids   # (N=1, T)
with torch.no_grad():
    out = model.generate(
        ids,
        do_sample=True,            # False -> greedy; True enables the controls below
        temperature=1.0,           # applied before top-k / top-p
        top_k=3,
        top_p=0.8,
        max_new_tokens=20,
        num_return_sequences=1,
        pad_token_id=tok.eos_token_id,
    )
print(tok.decode(out[0], skip_special_tokens=True))   # out shape (1, T+20)

# Greedy vs beam, for comparison:
with torch.no_grad():
    greedy = model.generate(ids, do_sample=False, max_new_tokens=20)
    beam = model.generate(ids, num_beams=4, do_sample=False, max_new_tokens=20)
print("greedy", tuple(greedy.shape), "beam", tuple(beam.shape))

Temperature 0 is not a valid replacement for top-\(k=1\): most libraries expect a positive temperature, while top_k=1 is the supported way to request greedy behaviour from a sampler. Apply temperature before truncation; truncating first and then scaling wastes the renormalisation you just performed.

Common mistakes

Temperature 0 vs top-k 1

A zero temperature divides by zero; libraries either error or clamp it to a tiny value. If you want deterministic decoding, use do_sample=False or top_k=1 rather than \(\tau=0\).

top-p before temperature

Truncating at \(\tau=1\) and then heating the survivors gives a different distribution from heating first and then truncating. The correct order is softmax with temperature, then top-\(k\), then top-\(p\), renormalising after each cut.

Sampling without a seed

Unseeded draws are not reproducible, so a “regression” in output quality may just be a different random path. Fix a seed for evaluation and report it alongside the decoding parameters.

Comparing perplexity across tokenizers

A tokenizer that splits words into more tokens changes the per-token likelihood and therefore the perplexity. Only compare PPL between models that tokenise text the same way; otherwise compare a length-normalised task metric instead.

Debug this

A reader reports: “generation repeats one token forever, and the output never changes.” The model is fine; the decoding configuration is not: 

out = model.generate(ids, do_sample=True, top_k=1,
                     max_new_tokens=50, pad_token_id=tok.eos_token_id)
# BUG: top_k=1 is greedy decoding; repetition has no route to escape
Show the likely cause and the fix

top_k=1 keeps exactly one token at every step, so sampling is greedy in disguise. Once the model enters a repetitive loop, no alternative token can ever be drawn. Raise top_k (for example 40), or use top_p around 0.9, and add a repetition penalty such as repetition_penalty=1.1. Compare a few seeds: if all outputs are identical while do_sample=True, your truncation has collapsed the distribution to a point mass.

Quick detector: print the number of non-zero probabilities after truncation. If it is 1, you are decoding greedily regardless of what the sampling flag says.

Summary

1 · A causal decoder factorises sequences as \(p(x_{1:T})=\prod_t p(x_t\mid x_{<t})\); for \((0.4,0.3,0.2)\) the product is \(0.024\), giving perplexity \(e^{3.7297/3}=3.467\).
2 · Temperature divides logits before softmax (\(\tau=0.5\) raises the top probability from \(0.5630\) to \(0.8292\)); top-\(k\) and top-\(p\) then cut the tail and must renormalise (\(0.5630/0.8958=0.6285\)).
3 · Greedy and beam search are deterministic, sampling needs a seed, and beam score is \((1/L)\sum\log p\); never compare perplexity across tokenizers.

Cheat sheet — M4·04

  • \(p(x_{1:T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p(x_t|x_{<t})\). Worked: 0.4·0.3·0.2 = 0.024.
  • \(\mathrm{PPL}=\exp(-\frac1T\sum_t\log p_t)\). Worked: exp(3.7297/3) = 3.467.
  • \(\mathrm{softmax}(z/\tau)\): logits (2,1,0.5,0,−1) → p = (0.5630, 0.2071, 0.1256, 0.0762, 0.0280) at τ=1.
  • Top-p: keep the shortest prefix reaching p, divide by S. Worked: 0.8 needs 3 tokens, S=0.8958, first p=0.6285.
  • Beam: \(s=(1/L)\sum_\ell\log p_\ell\); compare −1.2432 with −1.0337 for two length-3 candidates.
  • Order of operations: temperature → top-k → top-p → seeded draw; top-k=1 is greedy.
